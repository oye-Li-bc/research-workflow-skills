# A small paper-to-talk ledger

| Talk claim | Paper location | Experiment scope | Slide use |
|---|---|---|---|
| What problem is addressed? | Abstract or introduction | Task and assumptions | Motivation |
| What changes in the method? | Method section and diagram | Inputs, modules, outputs | Method |
| What was measured? | Table/figure identifier | Dataset, hardware, baseline | Results |
| Where does it fail? | Limitations or reported failure | Tested boundary | Discussion |

For a short talk, spend most time on one method diagram and a small number of
results. Define symbols before using an equation. Separate paper-reported numbers
from the presenter's reproduction. Choose fewer slides when the talk budget is
small; speaker rehearsal is the best evidence that the deck fits.

For layout, inspect a dense method frame, an equation, the widest table, and
figure captions. Shorten or split content before shrinking everything.
